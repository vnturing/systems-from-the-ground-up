## State is the truth; notifications are hints
A condition variable lets a thread wait for a predicate protected by a mutex. `pthread_cond_wait` atomically releases the mutex and waits, then reacquires the mutex before returning. Atomicity here closes the check-to-sleep gap relative to other participants following the same locking protocol. [[cond]]

A consumer typically waits while `count == 0 && !closed`. If items exist, it removes one under the mutex. If the queue is closed and empty, it terminates. The loop is essential: wakeups can be spurious, and another consumer may acquire the mutex first and take the available item.

## A bounded queue needs two predicates
A producer waits while `count == capacity && !closed`. A consumer waits while `count == 0 && !closed`. After insertion, a consumer may now proceed; after removal, a producer may now proceed. Separate not-empty and not-full condition variables make these reasons explicit.

Set and test the predicates under the same mutex. Signaling without a state change does not create an item. A signal sent before a thread begins waiting is not stored as a future item count. This is why condition variables and counting semaphores are different abstractions. OSTEP's condition-variable chapter develops the reasoning. [[cv]]

## Closing is part of the queue interface
Choose graceful drain semantics: close prevents new submissions, wakes blocked participants, and lets consumers drain already queued items before returning “done.” A broadcast on close lets every waiter recheck its termination condition. Destroy mutexes and condition variables only after all threads have stopped using them.

Alternative cancellation semantics may discard queued work, but must say who releases each discarded item's resources. In either design, shutdown is a state transition with ownership consequences, not a final `free` added after the happy path.

## Test the awkward schedules
Test capacity one, more consumers than items, close on an empty queue, close after production, and bursts that fill the queue. Track unique item identities to prove exactly-once consumption. A sum alone can miss a duplicated value paired with a missing value; Lab 08 checks count, sum, and a per-item seen array after joining.

::: deep A wait graph explains hanging tests
A producer blocked on full needs a consumer to remove an item. A consumer blocked on empty needs an insertion or close. If every worker is waiting, inspect those predicates and the thread expected to make them true. A missing close can strand consumers after the producer exits, even though every mutex operation was individually legal.

Keep the protected critical sections short, but do not split the predicate check from the wait. The lock's scope should match the invariant and transition, not a rule such as “only one line per lock.” Established lock primitives have progress and fairness considerations beyond mutual exclusion. [[locks]]
:::

## Retrieve before looking
A queue is empty and open. Two consumers wait. The producer inserts one item and broadcasts. Can both consumers remove an item after waking? Trace the mutex acquisition order.

::: solution Worked feedback
No. One consumer acquires the mutex first, sees the item, and removes it. The second reacquires later, sees empty, and waits again unless close has changed the termination predicate. An `if` in place of `while` could let the second remove nonexistent work. The notification did not reserve an item for each waiter.
:::
